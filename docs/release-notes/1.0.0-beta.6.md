# Gusset – Pattern Drafter 1.0.0 beta 6

Gusset is a free program for drafting sewing patterns in 2D, with a 3D window where you can sew the patterns together and try them on an avatar. Everything runs on your own computer, and your patterns stay there.

This beta is about **how Gusset feels to use**: undo works the plain way again, a view cube shows where you are looking from in 3D, folding a draped garment keeps its wrinkles, the avatar's outline can stand behind your patterns in 2D, and the colors are calmer. It is still a test version, so **please save your work often**.

**The easiest way to get it is the website:** [gussetdrafter.com](https://gussetdrafter.com/download) offers the right file for your computer, and you can also try Gusset in your browser with nothing to install.

## The first start

The builds are not signed yet, so Windows and macOS warn you the first time. [Install help](https://gussetdrafter.com/help) shows what to click:

- **Windows:** in "Windows protected your PC", click **More info**, then **Run anyway**.
- **Mac:** click **Done**, then System Settings ▸ Privacy & Security ▸ **Open Anyway**. If macOS says the app is damaged, run this in Terminal: `xattr -cr "/Applications/Gusset – Pattern Drafter.app"`
- **Linux:** allow the AppImage to run as a program. On Ubuntu 22.04 or newer you may need `libfuse2` (`libfuse2t64` on 24.04).

## If you tested a beta before

- **Your settings, library and unsaved work stay where they were.** Files from every earlier beta open as they were.
- **The file format has not changed since beta 5,** so files saved in beta 6 open in beta 5 too. The one thing beta 5 does not know is the avatar outline's position (below): it opens the file without it.
- **Files saved in beta 5 or 6 may not open in beta 4 or older.** Keep a copy saved by your old beta if you need to go back to it.
- **Beta 1 to beta 5 tell you about this version** when they start, with an **Update available** button at the top right. You can also choose Help ▸ Check for Updates….
- **The 0.9.0 betas do not.** Download this version once by hand. If you imported project files with one of them, their grainlines may be turned by 90°: choose **File ▸ Import ▸ Re-read Grainlines from Original File…** and pick the original `.zprj` or `.zpac`.

## What's new since 1.0.0 beta 5

### Undo is plain undo again

- **Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y** (and Edit ▸ Undo / Redo, and the arrows in the History panel) work as in any program: undo a few steps, make a change, and the undone steps are gone. No question is asked.
- **The question "keep the old steps or delete them?" now comes only when you go back from the History panel** (a double-click on a card, or a card's Go to This Step). Then the old steps can still be kept as a branch, as in beta 5.

### Finding your way in 3D

- **A view cube** sits in the 3D window's top right corner, in place of the eight view buttons. It turns with the view and shows where you are looking from.
  - Each face carries its name: FRONT, BACK, LEFT, RIGHT, TOP, BOTTOM. The part under the pointer turns orange and is named under the cube.
  - **Click** a face, an edge or a corner to glide to that view. Edges and corners give the in-between views, such as 3/4 Left.
  - Looking straight at a face, **small arrows** by its edges step to the next face. They show only while the pointer is over the cube.
  - **Drag** on the cube to turn the view.
  - **The little house** at the cube's corner goes back to the start view.
  - An **X / Y / Z** mark on one corner shows the directions. A line that lies behind the cube is dashed.
  - The number keys for the views (2, 8, 4, 6 and the others) still work, and so do the menu rows.
- **The floor is a plain grid** again, with no letters or colored lines on it.
- **The move handle (gizmo) now starts in World:** its arrows stay level with the floor and up stays up, whatever angle you look from. Gizmo ▸ Screen and Local are still there, and Gusset remembers your choice.

### Folding a draped garment

- **Fold Arrangement keeps the drape.** Click an internal line, a base line or a **seam** on the cloth: a ring appears with a handle on each side, a blue ball marked **1** and an orange cube marked **2**.
  - **Drag a handle** to turn only that side about the line, live, with all its wrinkles as they were. The other side stays exactly where it is.
  - On a seam, the patterns on each side of the seam turn, as if the seam were a hinge.
  - The angle is shown under each handle. Hold **Shift** to turn in steps of 15°.
  - **Right-click** for a box with both angles. The cloth follows as you type. OK keeps it; Esc puts both sides back.
  - Each turn is one step, so Ctrl+Z puts the cloth back exactly. The folded cloth is saved with the file like any other drape.

### The avatar's outline in 2D

- **The avatar can stand behind your patterns in the 2D window,** as a light grey outline at true size. Turn it on from the 2D window's display list (Avatar Outline), or right-click empty space ▸ More ▸ Avatar Outline. Choose Front, Back or Side.
  - It follows the avatar's height, size and pose. It is never printed or exported.
  - **Include Objects** adds the avatar's other parts, such as hair.
- **The avatar's waist sits on the 2D origin's horizontal line,** for any avatar and any size, so you can draft a waistline against it.
- **Edit Avatar Outline** (the last row of Avatar Outline): drag the outline up or down. Right-click it to type an exact offset or to reset it. Press Esc or click elsewhere when you are done. The position is saved in the file, and Ctrl+Z undoes it.

### The background image

- **The background image only reacts while you edit it.** Clicks and drags on it otherwise go to your patterns or select as usual, so you can no longer move it by accident. That is why the Lock row is gone.
- **To change it:** right-click empty space ▸ **Edit Background Image**. A frame with handles appears: drag it to move it, drag a corner to scale it, drag the ring above it to turn it.
- **Right-click the image while editing** for Scale…, Opacity…, Rotate…, Move/Position…, Replace Image… and Delete Background Image. Each box shows the change as you type; Delete also works with the Delete key.
- Press **Esc** or **Enter**, or click outside the image, to finish.

### Calmer colors

- **One accent color, the orange of the logo,** marks what is active everywhere: the tool in hand, the selected row in a list, the open menu, tabs, check boxes and sliders. The "not built yet" marks are grey now.
- The colors of the drawing itself (the yellow selection, the blue hover, the maps) are unchanged.
- **The History button** in the bottom bar is bigger and has its own icon, the tip of a seam ripper. It turns orange while the panel is open.
- The pointer's position in the bottom bar keeps a fixed width, so nothing beside it jumps as the numbers change.

## Known issues

- **Fold Arrangement turns the cloth as it hangs and does not simulate.** Press Simulate afterwards if you want the folded part to settle. Folds made in a file from an older beta still lay the pattern out flat, as before.
- **The avatar's waist is found automatically** (the narrowest place between the hips and the chest). On your own avatar, if no waist can be found, the outline is placed by an estimate: use Edit Avatar Outline to move it.
- **Changing an early step does not yet update the later steps.** If you go back from the History panel, change something and keep the old steps as a branch, that branch stays exactly as it was: it is not redone on top of your change. That comes in a later version.
- **Split Off copies do not follow the original.** Changing the original group later does not change the copies, and the other way round.
- **Going back in one group** takes along that group's patterns and the seams, topstitches and marks between them. Sewing from the group to a pattern outside it stays as it is.
- **How the cloth hung is kept only at Save States** and at a few other places. Going to any other step says "This step's cloth was not kept: Re-drape or Simulate", and nothing starts by itself.
- **The history keeps at most 500 steps.** Older steps are dropped, but never Save States or the points where a branch starts.
- **The simulation is a proof of concept.** It shows roughly how a garment hangs. It is not accurate enough to judge fit.
- **A pulled sleeve still stops about 10 cm forward**, because the arm is inside it. The avatar's arm itself does not move.
- **Seamless Blocking changes only the look.** Each section shows its fabric, but the whole pattern simulates with the pattern's own fabric.
- **Re-drape** does not carry cloth that hangs more than about 15 cm from the body. **Auto 3D Arrangement** is not built yet.
- **Simulation Quality is a setting of your computer**, not of the file. A file simulated in High Accuracy on one computer simulates in Normal on another unless it is switched on there too.
- **Once patterns are graded, the base size cannot be changed.**
- **Symmetric sewing** works only on a Symmetric Pattern copy (not an Instance copy), and only when the pattern has exactly one such copy. Zippers are not mirrored, and neither are seams on internal shapes of an unfolded pattern.
- **Convert to Handle Curve** still makes curve handles. It stays because Curve Grading works only on handle curves.
- **The path tracer gets ready again at every start of the program**, about 20 seconds on a mid-range graphics card. On cards that cannot do this in the background, it happens when you click Final Render.
- **Without a graphics card,** Final Render uses Quick. Path Traced can take many minutes for a large picture.
- **On some graphics cards the real size limit for a picture is only found by trying.** If a render fails with "the graphics card gave … pixels", the limit is lowered for the session: try again.
- **The Studio High Contrast light preset is much darker path traced** than in Quick. Burn Value brightens it. A path-traced picture can show a horizon line where the floor meets the background.
- **A saved turntable video needs the Render tab in front** while it is written.
- **A file saved in a browser** keeps a Particle Distance under 10 mm, but its saved drape is the browser's coarser one until it is simulated in the desktop app.
- **The fabric presets, the size run and parts of the skirt block are placeholder values,** to be replaced by real ones.
- **DXF output has not been tried on a cutter.**
- **Printing true to size depends on printing at 100 %.** Measure the 50 mm square on the first sheet.
- The project-file import is known for files written in 2026 only.
- On Apple-chip Macs, a fold line at 270° settles a little further from its target than elsewhere, and a fold's crease can look wavy on any computer.
