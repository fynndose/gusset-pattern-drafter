# Gusset – Pattern Drafter 1.0.0 beta 5

Gusset is a free program for drafting sewing patterns in 2D, with a 3D window where you can sew the patterns together and try them on an avatar. Everything runs on your own computer, and your patterns stay there.

This beta is about **the history**: a History panel that shows every step you have made, lets you go back to any of them, and keeps the variants you try instead of throwing them away. You can also give parts of a project their own history, so the shirt can go back to an earlier version while the trousers stay as they are. It is still a test version, so **please save your work often**.

**The easiest way to get it is the website:** [gussetdrafter.com](https://gussetdrafter.com/download) offers the right file for your computer, and you can also try Gusset in your browser with nothing to install.

## The first start

The builds are not signed yet, so Windows and macOS warn you the first time. [Install help](https://gussetdrafter.com/help) shows what to click:

- **Windows:** in "Windows protected your PC", click **More info**, then **Run anyway**.
- **Mac:** click **Done**, then System Settings ▸ Privacy & Security ▸ **Open Anyway**. If macOS says the app is damaged, run this in Terminal: `xattr -cr "/Applications/Gusset – Pattern Drafter.app"`
- **Linux:** allow the AppImage to run as a program. On Ubuntu 22.04 or newer you may need `libfuse2` (`libfuse2t64` on 24.04).

## What's new since 1.0.0 beta 4

### The History panel

- **Press D, or click HISTORY in the bottom bar** (also Edit ▸ History and Window ▸ History). The panel opens across the bottom of the window. Drag its top edge to make it taller; it remembers the height.
- **Every step is a card**, with the tool you used and what you did. Drag empty space to move around, and use the wheel to zoom.
  - A click only selects a card.
  - A **double-click on a card** takes your project back (or forward) to that step.
  - A **double-click on a card's name** renames it.
  - Many of the same step in a row (moving one point ten times, say) fold into one card "×10". Click the number to open it.
- **The history is much longer**: up to 500 steps, in the program and in the saved file.

### Going back without losing anything

- **Go back a few steps and change something:** Gusset asks whether to **keep the old steps as a branch** or **delete them**.
  - Keep them, and they hang below as a branch. Double-click the branch's last card to work on that version again. This is a good way to try out variants.
  - Delete asks once more, because it cannot be undone.
  - Tick **Remember my choice** to keep without being asked. Settings ▸ Preferences ▸ Keep the Old Future Without Asking switches that off again.
- **Save States:** right-click a card ▸ **Add Save State…** and give it a name, such as "First fitting". It shows as a little flag. A Save State is never dropped from the history, and it keeps how the cloth hung at that step in the file.
- **Condense History** (Edit menu, or the panel's header) keeps only the important steps: the start, Save States, branches and where you are now. It shows the size before and after. It makes big files smaller.
- **The history can be switched off for a project:** Edit ▸ Keep History for This Project. Undo still works as before, but nothing of the history goes into the file. When you start a new project, Gusset asks whether to keep a history. Settings ▸ Preferences ▸ History for New Projects lets you choose once for all.

### History Groups

- **Give part of a project its own history.** Select patterns, right-click one ▸ More ▸ **Create History Group…** and name it, for example "Shirt".
  - The History panel then shows a band, or lane, for each group. Each band has its own color, a shape, and its name.
  - Everything else is in the band called **Shared**.
- **Go back in one group only.** Double-click a step in the Shirt's band, and only the shirt's patterns go back to that step: the trousers stay as they are. This is a step of its own ("⤺ Shirt: …"), so **Ctrl+Z** undoes it as usual.
- When you change the shirt after going back, Gusset asks **keep or delete** for the shirt's later steps only.
- **Right-click a band's name** to rename the group or ungroup it.
- **Split Off** (right-click a step in a group's band): copies of the group's patterns, as they were at that step, hang on the pointer until you click them down. They become a new group of their own, linked by a dotted line to the step they came from. Seams between the group's own patterns come along.
- **Cut Off Branch** (right-click a step): the group's history starts at that step from now on, and its earlier steps are hidden. Ctrl+Z still goes back through them, and Condense History removes them for good.

### A cleaner window

- **The bottom bar shows only** the window layout buttons, the pointer's position, the unit and HISTORY. The position no longer makes anything jump about as the numbers change. What was there before is still there, elsewhere:
  - **Zoom:** in the 2D window's title ("2D Pattern Window · 100 %").
  - **Grid snap:** Settings ▸ Preferences ▸ Snap to Grid.
  - **Seam allowance on or off:** Settings ▸ Display ▸ 2D Pattern ▸ Seam Allowance.
  - **Avatar:** Avatar ▸ Load Avatar (Ctrl+Shift+A).
  - **Tools that are not built yet:** Settings ▸ Show Tools That Are Not Built Yet.
  - **Check Sewing Length:** on the 2D toolbar, in 2D ▸ Sewing, and on Edit Sewing's right-click. Its limits are in Settings ▸ Check Sewing Length Limits….
  - **Print Layout's overlaps:** in Print Layout's title.
  - **The tool hints:** Settings ▸ Preferences ▸ Show Tool Hints (off at first).
- **A small ● after the file's name** in the 3D window's title means there are unsaved changes. It goes when you save. If the automatic backup ever fails, a warning still shows in the bottom bar.

### Safer work

- **Your own avatar now survives a crash.** If Gusset closes without saving and you choose Recover at the next start, your own avatar comes back too, posed as it was, with the history and the cloth.

## Known issues

- **Changing an early step does not yet update the later steps.** If you go back, change something and keep the old steps as a branch, that branch stays exactly as it was: it is not redone on top of your change. That comes in a later version.
- **Split Off copies do not follow the original.** Changing the original group later does not change the copies, and the other way round.
- **Going back in one group** takes along that group's patterns and the seams, topstitches and marks between them. Sewing from the group to a pattern outside it stays as it is.
- **How the cloth hung is kept only at Save States** and at a few other places. Going to any other step says "This step's cloth was not kept: Re-drape or Simulate", and nothing starts by itself.
- **The history keeps at most 500 steps.** Older steps are dropped, but never Save States or the points where a branch starts.
- **The simulation is a proof of concept.** It shows roughly how a garment hangs. It is not accurate enough to judge fit.
- **A pulled sleeve still stops about 10 cm forward**, because the arm is inside it. The avatar's arm itself does not move.
- **Seamless Blocking changes only the look.** Each section shows its fabric, but the whole pattern simulates with the pattern's own fabric.
- **Re-drape** does not carry cloth that hangs more than about 15 cm from the body. **Auto 3D Arrangement** is not built yet.
- **Simulation Quality is a setting of your computer**, not of the file. A file simulated in High Accuracy on one computer simulates in Normal on another unless it is switched on there too.
- **Once patterns are graded, the base size cannot be changed.**
- **Symmetric sewing** works only on a Symmetric Pattern copy (not an Instance copy), and only when the pattern has exactly one such copy. Zippers are not mirrored, and neither are seams on internal shapes of an unfolded pattern.
- **Convert to Handle Curve** still makes curve handles. It stays because Curve Grading works only on handle curves.
- **The path tracer gets ready again at every start of the program**, about 20 seconds on a mid-range graphics card. On cards that cannot do this in the background, it happens when you click Final Render.
- **Without a graphics card,** Final Render uses Quick. Path Traced can take many minutes for a large picture.
- **On some graphics cards the real size limit for a picture is only found by trying.** If a render fails with "the graphics card gave … pixels", the limit is lowered for the session: try again.
- **The Studio High Contrast light preset is much darker path traced** than in Quick. Burn Value brightens it. A path-traced picture can show a horizon line where the floor meets the background.
- **A saved turntable video needs the Render tab in front** while it is written.
- **A file saved in a browser** keeps a Particle Distance under 10 mm, but its saved drape is the browser's coarser one until it is simulated in the desktop app.
- **The fabric presets, the size run and parts of the skirt block are placeholder values,** to be replaced by real ones.
- **DXF output has not been tried on a cutter.**
- **Printing true to size depends on printing at 100 %.** Measure the 50 mm square on the first sheet.
- The project-file import is known for files written in 2026 only.
- On Apple-chip Macs, a fold line at 270° settles a little further from its target than elsewhere, and a fold's crease can look wavy on any computer.
