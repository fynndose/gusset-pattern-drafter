# Gusset – Pattern Drafter 1.0.0 beta 3

Gusset is a free program for drafting sewing patterns in 2D, with a 3D window where you can sew the patterns together and try them on an avatar. Everything runs on your own computer, and your patterns stay there.

This beta is mostly fixes from the last round of testing: angles and curve points in 2D, sewing both sides of a symmetric pattern at once, keeping your own avatar and the drape in the file, a faster and cleaner start, and no more freezing when the Render workspace opens. It is still a test version, so **please save your work often**.

**The easiest way to get it is the website:** [gussetdrafter.com](https://gussetdrafter.com/download) offers the right file for your computer, and you can also try Gusset in your browser with nothing to install.

## The first start

The builds are not signed yet, so Windows and macOS warn you the first time. [Install help](https://gussetdrafter.com/help) shows what to click:

- **Windows:** in "Windows protected your PC", click **More info**, then **Run anyway**.
- **Mac:** click **Done**, then System Settings ▸ Privacy & Security ▸ **Open Anyway**. If macOS says the app is damaged, run this in Terminal: `xattr -cr "/Applications/Gusset – Pattern Drafter.app"`
- **Linux:** allow the AppImage to run as a program. On Ubuntu 22.04 or newer you may need `libfuse2` (`libfuse2t64` on 24.04).

- **Your settings, library and unsaved work stay where they were.** Your files open as they were.
- **A file saved by this version does not open in beta 2 or older.** The file format changed for symmetric sewing (below). Keep a copy saved by beta 2 if you need to go back.

## What's new since 1.0.0 beta 2

### Editing in 2D

- **Angles update while you drag** a point, a line or a curve point: at the point you move and at its two neighbors. Before, they only changed when you let go.
- **Internal lines show their angles too**: at a bend, at a free end (to level) and where a line meets the outline.
- **You can type an angle at a point**: in the Property Editor under Selected Point, or in Moving Distance. Choose which of the two lines keeps its length.
- **Ctrl while dragging a point** now keeps it on one of its own lines, never on a diagonal, and shows that line as a thin guide.
- **Curve points:** with Edit Curve Point, drag a box or Shift+click to select several, and Delete removes them all in one step. Deleting a line's last curve point makes it straight.
- **No more stray curve handles.** The Ellipse tools, Smooth Curve, Fullness, Offset as Internal Line, DXF import and the rest now make curve points. In older files, drag a handle onto its own point, or select it and press Delete, to remove it.
- **Clicking a pattern no longer brings it to the front.** Use the Order keys: Ctrl+] and Ctrl+[ one step, Ctrl+Shift+] and Ctrl+Shift+[ to the front or back. They go by the key's position, so on a German keyboard they are Ctrl++ and Ctrl+Ü. Over the 3D window, Ctrl+[ and Ctrl+] are still Superimpose.
- **Trace, Shape mode:** right-click an internal line for **Cut** and **Cut & Sew**; right-click a closed internal shape for **Convert to Hole**.

### Sewing

- **Symmetric sewing:** sew one side of a symmetric pair (a pattern and its Symmetric Pattern copy, or an unfolded pattern) and the other side is sewn too. The two seams stay tied: change or delete one and the other follows. To separate them: Edit Sewing, right-click the seam ▸ More ▸ **Unlink Symmetric Sewing**.

### 3D

- **Your own avatar is saved in the pattern file**, posed as you left it, so the file reopens on the same avatar with the garment still draped. The file grows by the size of the avatar (about 0.6 MB for one like Marty). **This means the avatar travels with the file:** if your avatar is a scan of a real person, think before you share the file. Marty and Jessica are only named in the file, not stored.
- **Editing a draped pattern in 2D keeps the drape.** Lowering a neckline, adding width at a hem, or moving or turning the pattern in 2D no longer sends it back to its starting place. New fabric starts beside the cloth that was there; simulate again to settle it. Only Reset 3D Arrangement takes it back.
- **Pulling the cloth while simulating** follows the mouse more closely, and the cloth stays where you let go unless the garment pulls it back.

### Starting and rendering

- **A clean start.** The splash stays until the program is fully ready, then the window appears already drawn. No more grey window.
- **The portable `.exe` shows a picture while it unpacks** instead of nothing for 15 seconds.
- **Opening the Render workspace no longer freezes the program.** On some graphics cards it held everything for 20 to 40 seconds. On those cards Path Traced now gets ready when you click Final Render, with a bar and a Cancel button, and the program keeps answering.
- **Render and 3D snapshot sizes are capped at what your graphics card can draw.** The limit is shown beside the size fields. Larger pictures used to come out wrong without a warning. A file that asks for more renders at the limit, says so in one line, and keeps its own size.

### In a browser

Some things are too heavy for a browser tab and could crash it, so in a browser they are the desktop app's only: **Path Traced**, a **Particle Distance under 10 mm**, and **pictures over 4096 pixels a side**. They are shown with a lock, and choosing one tells you why, with a link to the free desktop app. Quick and Schematic rendering stay. A file that uses them still opens: the browser runs at its limits for the session, says so in one line, and the file keeps its own settings.

## Known issues

- **The simulation is a proof of concept.** It shows roughly how a garment hangs. It is not accurate enough to judge fit.
- **A garment can spring back after you pull it** while simulating, for example a T-shirt's collar, because it hangs on the shoulders. One tester saw a shirt stay stuck tilted back on the avatar; we could not make that happen. If it happens to you, please save the file and send it.
- **Unsaved work recovered after a crash** names your own avatar but does not hold it: load the avatar again to see the drape. Saved files hold it.
- **Symmetric sewing** works only on a Symmetric Pattern copy, not an Instance copy, and only when the pattern has exactly one such copy. Zippers are not mirrored, and neither are seams on internal shapes of an unfolded pattern. Seams that were already in a file, or came in with an import, are not mirrored.
- **Convert to Handle Curve** still makes curve handles. It stays because Curve Grading works only on handle curves.
- **The path tracer gets ready again at every start of the program**, about 20 seconds on a mid-range graphics card. On cards that cannot do this in the background, it happens when you click Final Render.
- **Without a graphics card,** Final Render uses Quick. Path Traced can take many minutes for a large picture.
- **On some graphics cards the real size limit is only found by trying.** If a render fails with "the graphics card gave … pixels", the limit is lowered for the session: try again.
- **The Studio High Contrast light preset is much darker path traced** than in Quick. Burn Value brightens it.
- **A path-traced picture can show a horizon line** where the floor meets the background.
- **A saved turntable video needs the Render tab in front** while it is written.
- **A file saved in a browser** keeps a Particle Distance under 10 mm, but its saved drape is the browser's coarser one until it is simulated in the desktop app.
- **The fabric presets, the size run and parts of the skirt block are placeholder values,** to be replaced by real ones.
- **DXF output has not been tried on a cutter.**
- **Printing true to size depends on printing at 100 %.** Measure the 50 mm square on the first sheet.
- The project-file import is known for files written in 2026 only.
- On Apple-chip Macs, a fold line at 270° settles a little further from its target than elsewhere.
